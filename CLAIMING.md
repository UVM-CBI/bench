# Claiming a Task

New to this? [ONBOARDING.md](ONBOARDING.md) is the gentler version. This is the
complete rules.

## Before you can claim anything

You need a free GitHub account, and you need to be in the **UVM-CBI**
organization on GitHub.

1. Sign up: <https://github.com/signup>. Use your UVM email — it also gets you
   the [student developer pack](https://education.github.com/pack).
2. Post your username in `#general` in the CBI Bench team.
3. Accept the org invitation that arrives by email.

**Do this when you join, not when you find a task you want.** Invitations may take a
day or two, and until you accept one you can't be assigned. You can browse and
read without any of it.

Two things that trip people up: the invite goes to whatever email is on your
GitHub account, which might not be your UVM one; and invites expire after a week.
If you miss it, post again in `#general`.

## The process

**1. Find one you can actually do.** Read the whole issue — deliverable,
background assumed, and deadline included. If you're not certain you have the
skill, that's totally fine, but say so when you claim. 

**2. Post in `#open-tasks` in the CBI Bench team.** Every new task gets posted
there automatically. Reply in that task's thread with:

- How you'd approach it — two or three sentences, enough to show you read the
  issue and have a plan
- When you'd have it done

That's the whole application. Examples:

> I'd design this in OnShape starting from the diagram file linked in the issue, and
> check the fit against the printed housing before finalizing. Done by Oct 14.

**3. Wait to be assigned.** You'll get assigned on GitHub and the issue picks up
a `claimed` label. Don't start work first — occasionally multiple people
might claim the same thing.

If you're not in the org yet and don't want to wait, comment one word on the
issue itself. That makes you assignable for that issue. Still post your username
in `#general` so it's sorted for next time.

**4. Do the work.** Ask questions **on the GitHub issue**, not in Teams and not
by email. Everything about a task should be findable in one place, including by
whoever picks it up if you hand it off.

**5. Deliver.** Put files where the issue specifies — OneDrive, OnShape,
wherever — and paste the link in an issue comment. Then add the `needs-review`
label. Nothing gets committed to this repo: not CAD, not powerpoints, not
video, not design files.

## Rules

**Two weeks of no activity releases your claim.** An assigned issue with no activity
for 14 days gets automatically unassigned and returned to the pool. This is not a
punishment — it's how the board stays updated about what's actually being worked
on.

Avoiding it is easy, e.g.: "Still on this, waiting on an email,
back to it Monday" keeps your task active and assigned to you.

Some longer tasks carry a `long-running` label and are exempt. The issue will
say.

**One at a time to start.** Close one out before claiming a second. After that,
claim as many as you can handle.

**Handing off is fine and is not a failure.** If you get busy, or it turns out to
be beyond what you can do, say so on the issue. Write down where you got to and
what you found. Someone else can pick it up. That's a genuinely helpful
outcome.

**Ask scope questions before you claim.** "Does this need to be manufacturable or
is a concept model fine?" and "How deep should the competitive scan go — five
companies or thirty?" are both good questions. Ask on the issue.

## What you get

- Public contribution history on your GitHub profile. You can also make your
  UVM-CBI org membership visible there if you want the badge.
- Named credit in whatever the work feeds into — paper, poster, etc.
- A reference from someone who has seen you finish something.

## Ownership of what you make

Most bench tasks carry no strings — you make it, you can show it, and it's
credited to you.

Some may be tied to unpublished work, a pending patent, or a sponsor agreement.
Those say so in the issue, and there's paperwork to sort before you start rather
than after. This applies to written work as much as to hardware: a market
analysis or a prior art search tied to a live disclosure is handled the same way
a CAD file would be.
